# SEO & GTM Research — CV Builder SaaS

*Strategy synthesis based on market/competitor research and general knowledge of zero-budget SaaS launch tactics in emerging markets. Search-volume-specific numbers (Google Keyword Planner data for Malawi) were not pulled in this pass — flagged as a gap; the channel and content strategy below don't depend on having exact volumes to start executing.*

## 1. SEO Opportunity

- **Search volume data**: not retrieved in this pass (would need direct Google Keyword Planner/Ads access, not a general web search). **Flag as a gap** — worth a 30-minute pass in Keyword Planner or a free tool (Ubersuggest, Google Trends for relative interest) before finalizing the content calendar, but not a blocker to starting.
- **Directionally winnable keywords for a new site**: broad terms like "CV builder" are dominated by global players (Zety, Canva, Novoresume) with huge domain authority — not winnable early. **Long-tail, Malawi-specific terms** are the real opportunity: "CV format Malawi", "how to write a CV for [specific Malawi employer type, e.g., NGO jobs Malawi]", "CV template Malawi graduate", "free CV maker Malawi". These have low competition precisely because no local competitor exists (per competitor research).
- **Content strategy**: blog/guide content built around these long-tail terms, plus dedicated template landing pages (e.g., "NGO CV template Malawi") that double as SEO entry points and in-product template previews.
- **Domain**: a **.com** domain is generally the safer default for credibility and shareability outside Malawi too (diaspora, future regional expansion) — a `.mw` domain could reinforce local trust but isn't necessary for basic local SEO effectiveness, and DNS/registration friction for `.mw` can be higher for a solo dev than for a global TLD. Recommend `.com` unless local brand signaling is a strong priority.
- **Local SEO**: a Google Business Profile is more suited to physical/local-service businesses; for a web SaaS the higher-leverage local-SEO move is simply ranking content pages for Malawi-specific long-tail queries and getting listed in relevant local directories/forums where job seekers already gather.

## 2. Launch Channels (Free)

| Channel | Tactic | Effort/Result |
|---|---|---|
| WhatsApp | Share directly into existing Malawian job-seeker and alumni WhatsApp groups (with permission/where appropriate) — this is the single highest-leverage channel for this specific audience given the persona research's confirmed WhatsApp-centric habits | Low effort, potentially high result — the core channel, not a side channel |
| Facebook groups | Post in Malawian job-seeker/graduate Facebook groups (identify real, active ones rather than guessing generic group names) | Low effort, moderate-high result |
| University partnerships | Approach career centers/student unions directly (UNIMA, MUBAS, Mzuzu University, etc.) with a free-tier offer for their graduating class | Moderate effort (requires outreach/relationship building), potentially high result and durable (recurring graduating cohorts) |
| Reddit (r/Malawi and similar) | Smaller, less active audience for this specific niche than WhatsApp/Facebook in Malawi — worth a light presence, not a primary bet | Low effort, low-moderate result |
| LinkedIn | Useful for reaching the NGO/government-track persona (Patricia) and for credibility signaling to partners, less so as a mass acquisition channel for the broader fresh-grad audience | Low effort, moderate result for a specific sub-segment |
| TikTok | Growing across Africa; short "CV tips" content could work well given the audience skews young, but requires consistent content production the solo-dev founder may not have bandwidth for at launch | Moderate effort, uncertain but potentially high upside |
| YouTube | Longer-form CV tutorial content doubles as SEO asset and channel; lower priority than WhatsApp/Facebook at launch given production time cost | Moderate-high effort, moderate/slow-building result |
| Twitter/X | Lower relative usage for this specific demographic versus WhatsApp/Facebook in Malawi — low priority | Low priority |
| Church/community groups | Consistent with the persona research's noted role of church/community networks as informal CV-help sources today — a believable, low-cost distribution point via word of mouth | Low effort, moderate result, hard to measure |
| NGO partnerships (youth employment programs) | The AfDB-funded "Jobs for Youth" program (per market research) and similar NGO youth-employment initiatives are a natural partnership target — they need exactly this kind of tool for their beneficiaries | Higher effort (relationship-building, possibly a pitch/proposal), potentially high and durable result |

**Prioritization given a solo dev with $0 budget**: WhatsApp + Facebook groups + one or two university partnerships first; layer in TikTok/YouTube content only if bandwidth allows after the core product and launch channels are working.

## 4. Referral & Virality

- Honest assessment: a CV builder has **low natural virality** — using a CV builder isn't inherently a shareable moment the way, say, a design tool's output is. Don't over-invest here.
- A modest **referral incentive** (e.g., extra free export or a small discount for both referrer and referee) can still help, especially layered onto the already-social WhatsApp-driven discovery pattern.
- **Watermark on free PDFs**: a reasonable idea and a common pattern among competitors (implicitly, given their freemium models) — it creates gentle, non-annoying visibility every time a free CV is shared/submitted, without being so aggressive it damages the free user's job application (keep it small/discreet, not covering content).
- **Public shareable CV links** (à la Hello.cv, per competitor research): a plausible V2 feature with real differentiation value, not an MVP requirement.

## 5. Partnerships

- **Universities**: approach career centers/student unions directly, offering a free tier or bulk-access deal for graduating classes — the persona research shows this is exactly where students currently get informal CV help, so it's a natural insertion point.
- **Employers who hire fresh grads**: unlikely to sponsor a new, unproven tool at launch — deprioritize until there's traction/data to pitch with.
- **Career fairs and job expos**: worth attending or getting a presence at if any occur in reach, given they concentrate exactly the target audience in one place.
- **NGOs/government programs** (the AfDB "Jobs for Youth" project, TEVETA, youth funds): a credible, mission-aligned partnership angle — pitch the tool as free/discounted infrastructure for their existing beneficiary programs rather than a standalone commercial pitch.

## 6. Pricing & Positioning Launch

- Given the subscription-avoidance recommendation in the PayChangu research (a one-time/time-boxed Pro pass fits the platform and the user's actual need better than recurring billing), position launch pricing as a **simple one-time unlock**, not a subscription — this is also easier to communicate to a price-sensitive, subscription-wary audience.
- A **"founding user" launch price** (a discounted one-time price for the first cohort, framed as a launch offer) is a reasonable lever to drive urgency and initial conversions without needing full freemium-tier engineering at MVP.
- **Free tier** should let a user build and preview a complete CV (removing the single biggest current pain point — not knowing if it looks professional) with payment gating only the clean, watermark-free export — consistent with where the persona research shows the confidence-building moment happens.
- Exact MWK price point: a launch decision to validate against real signups, not something to fix from research alone (see the willingness-to-pay gap flagged in research-market.md).

## 7. Early Metrics

- **Week 1**: signups, % who complete a CV (start-to-finish completion rate — a proxy for onboarding friction).
- **Month 1**: total signups vs. the 100-signup MVP target, free→paid conversion rate vs. the 10% target, CV completion rate vs. the 60% target (all three already defined as MVP success metrics in 00-README.md — this is where you actually track them).
- **Month 3**: retention/return-usage (do users come back to update a CV for a second application?), and channel-level attribution (which of WhatsApp/Facebook/university partnerships actually drove signups) to decide where to double down.
- **North star metric candidate**: completed, exported CVs — it captures both product value delivery and is a natural proxy for revenue (since export/download is the paywall moment).
- **Leading indicators of PMF**: unprompted shares/referrals from users, unprompted requests for features (e.g., NGO-specific templates, Chichewa support) surfacing organically in support conversations, and returning users building a second CV for a new application.

## 8. Competitive Moats (medium-term)

- **Local trust/brand**: being visibly Malawi-first (local pricing, local payment methods, local support) is a moat none of the researched global or regional competitors can easily replicate quickly.
- **University/NGO partnerships**: durable, hard-to-replicate distribution once established (recurring graduating cohorts, ongoing beneficiary programs).
- **Content library**: the 20-post SEO content library compounds over time and is slow for a fast-follow competitor to replicate from scratch.
- **Community**: not a launch-stage moat, but a plausible medium-term one if a job-seeker community layer is added later (consistent with what Fuzu does regionally).
- **Data network effects**: not really applicable to a CV builder in the way it is for, say, a marketplace — deprioritize as a moat thesis.

## 9. 90-Day GTM Roadmap (illustrative)

| Weeks | Focus | Key actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1-2 | Pre-launch prep | Finalize free/paid split, publish 3-5 seed blog posts, prep WhatsApp/Facebook group outreach list |
| 3-4 | Soft launch | Launch to a small seed group (personal network, one university career center), collect direct feedback, fix onboarding friction |
| 5-8 | Public launch | Post in WhatsApp/Facebook groups, publish remaining blog posts on a steady cadence, begin outreach to 2-3 more universities and one NGO youth-employment program |
| 9-12 | Iterate & expand channels | Analyze month-1 metrics against MVP targets, double down on whichever channel drove the most signups, consider a founding-user price adjustment based on real conversion data, begin light TikTok/YouTube content if bandwidth allows |

## First 100 Users Playbook (step-by-step)

1. Ship the MVP with free build + paid export, and the initial template set validated against persona research (standard one-pager + NGO/government-style option).
2. Personally share in 5-10 real, active Malawian job-seeker/graduate WhatsApp and Facebook groups, with a short, honest post (not spammy) explaining what it is and that it's free to try.
3. Reach out directly to 2-3 university career centers with a simple pitch: free access for their current graduating class in exchange for feedback and a mention to students.
4. Publish the first 5 blog posts from the content list, optimized around the long-tail keywords identified above.
5. Watch the three MVP success metrics daily; talk directly (WhatsApp/DM) to the first 10-20 signups to find onboarding friction before scaling outreach further.
6. Once >60% CV completion rate and initial paid conversions are confirmed, expand outreach to more groups/universities and begin the NGO partnership conversation.
